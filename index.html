The arithmetic nobody shows you up front

A tracker at $9.99 a month is about $120 a year, and roughly $360 over three years. More than most people spend on the phone they're logging on. Subscriptions aren't evil; some things genuinely need ongoing work to run. A food diary mostly needs to store the numbers you typed. That's a one-time problem, so it has a one-time price.

That figure is an illustration, not a claim about any particular app. Check what you're currently paying: your app store's subscription list will tell you in ten seconds.

What you actually get

A complete tracker, not a demo with the useful parts locked behind a subscription.

Logging

Log it in seconds, not minutes

Scan a barcode, search by name, or type it in yourself. Barcodes resolve against Open Food Facts and nutrition data against USDA FoodData Central, with the source printed on the screen so you can see where a number came from.

  • Amounts in grams, ounces, fluid ounces or servings, converted for you
  • Saved foods keep the values you corrected, and you can update them again
  • Meals group the day, and a saved meal logs again in one tap

Progress

Progress that tells you the truth

Calories against your target, a heat map of the month, and a weight trend over eight weeks. Underneath every chart is the number most apps leave out: how many days it was actually based on.

  • Switch between week, month and year views of the same data
  • Days you didn't log are shown as empty, never as failures
  • Best day and average calories, so a good week is visible at a glance

Micronutrients

Vitamins and minerals, with the gaps left visible

Calorie counting misses most of what food does. Fituous tracks the vitamins and minerals you care about, with your own targets and a running total against them.

  • Personal targets, marked for you where they differ from the default
  • Upper limits shown alongside targets, not hidden behind a warning
  • A vitamin with no data reads no data, because a gap is not a deficiency

Hydration

Water that knows your glass

Log a glass, a bottle or a flask, sized to your own containers rather than someone else's idea of a serving. Switch between millilitres and ounces whenever you like.

  • Your container sizes, saved as presets you tap
  • A fill-level meter instead of another number to interpret
  • The week's hydration at a glance, so a good run is obvious

Theming

Light and dark, included

The whole app follows your system theme. It is not a setting hidden behind a subscription, because a dark background is not a feature you should have to rent.

  • Every screen is designed in both, including the charts
  • No upsell, no "pro" theme pack, no nag to unlock it

Health platforms

It works with the health app your phone already has

Fituous reads your workouts and weigh-ins out of the platform your phone already keeps them in, and writes back what you log, so the two stay in step.

  • On iPhone that is the Apple Health app, through HealthKit
  • On Android that is Health Connect, which ships with Android 14 and up
  • Access is read and write, and you can revoke it in the health app whenever you like

Where your data goes: nowhere

Every claim here is a design decision in the app, not a policy paragraph.

No account

There is no sign-up, no email, no password. Your diary is stored on your device, so there is no server holding a copy and nothing to breach.

Voice stays on your phone

Speech is recognised on-device. Fituous refuses the server route outright: on a device that can't do on-device recognition, it says so instead of quietly uploading your voice.

No ads

No adverts, no ad SDKs, nothing to pay to make them stop. You already paid.

No tracking or analytics

No analytics, no crash telemetry, no cookies on this page either. Nobody is counting how long you looked at the subscription section.

Health data is read, never written

When you import workouts or weigh-ins from Health Connect or Apple Health, access is read-only. Fituous never adds to or changes your health records.

Public data stays public

Food data comes from Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central. Nothing derived from it is sold, resold, or bundled into anything for sale.

Questions people actually ask

Is there a subscription?

No. You buy Fituous once and every feature is yours. No trial that converts, no premium tier, no "pro" version waiting behind a paywall, and no recurring charge to forget about.

How much does it cost?

A single one-time purchase, priced by the app store for your region. There is no figure on this page because a number here would be wrong somewhere in the world, and because a one-time price doesn't change when you're not looking.

What happens to my diary if I stop using it?

It stays on your device, because that's where it always was. Nothing is deleted for non-payment, and there is no account that lapses.

Why does voice need on-device recognition?

Because the alternative sends your audio to a server. Both platforms default to that, so Fituous asks for the on-device path and declines to listen at all if it isn't available. On older Android versions that means voice logging is unavailable, which we'd rather tell you than quietly break the promise.

What's in beta?

Voice Builder, and it's labelled BETA wherever it appears. It's honest about its limits: it shows you what it heard and what each item resolved to before logging anything. Everything else is finished, shipped and included.

Which platforms?

iPhone and Android. There's no web app: the diary lives on the device, and a browser tab is the wrong place for it.

Is this medical advice?

No. Fituous counts what you tell it and shows you the arithmetic, including how much of the month you actually logged. It points out patterns, never causes, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor or a dietitian.
